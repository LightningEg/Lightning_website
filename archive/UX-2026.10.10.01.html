Sample profiles · 2026

Four Lives. Four Money Questions.

These fictional Egyptian profiles are designed to show how different habits and income patterns change the questions you ask of your money. Each sample covers January through October 2026 so far and can be loaded into an empty Lightning profile.

How to load a profile

A Few Clicks in Lightning.

Each built-in profile loads its accounts and sample activity for you.

  1. 1

    Open an Empty Profile

    On the Lightning welcome screen, create a new profile for sample data, or select an existing empty one. Samples cannot be added to a ledger that already has accounts.

  2. 2

    Choose a Story

    Select Amr, Abdelrahman, Menna or Fatma, then choose Load sample. Lightning creates the accounts and adds the fictional transactions. Abdelrahman’s equity fund trades are included too.

  3. 3

    Explore the Numbers

    Open Overview, Expenses, Budget, Investments or Cash planning to see the pattern behind that profile. Sample data stays in the profile you loaded.

Cash flow

Amr · The resourceful side hustler

Amr works an engineering job in Cairo and earns EGP 14,500 a month. He is quick to spot a resale or commission opportunity, but those payments are unpredictable: some months bring nothing, others add EGP 7,000–22,000. Rent, food, family support and bills keep arriving. A couple of stock purchases for a small resale deal make the timing harder.

Explore: which months run short, how much cash to keep back, and what changes if a side deal pays late.

Opening cash: EGP 28,000 · No debt assumed · Sample income is fictional.

Consistency

Abdelrahman · The steady engineer

Abdelrahman is a salaried engineer in Giza. His pay rises from EGP 32,000 to EGP 36,000 over the year, with a performance bonus. He keeps daily spending measured and invests around EGP 6,000–8,500 a month in a fictional equity fund. In June, a new laptop and an unplanned weekend push spending well above his normal month.

Explore: how a one-off overspend affects his savings rate and steady investment habit over time.

Opening cash: EGP 60,000 · Equity fund prices are illustrative, not a recommendation.

Safety

Menna · The freelance artist

Menna lives in Alexandria and illustrates for agencies and small businesses. Her invoices range from EGP 8,000 to EGP 33,000, with a few months where no client payment arrives. Materials, rent, transport and everyday costs continue between projects, so she draws on a modest cash reserve during quiet stretches. By October, only EGP 4,000 is set aside against a EGP 60,000 emergency-fund target.

Explore: how large a buffer helps bridge late invoices and how to pace spending after a good month.

Opening cash: EGP 60,000 · Client payments are irregular by design.

Emergency reserve

Fatma · The household organiser

Fatma works in Cairo and earns EGP 18,000 a month. She keeps track of groceries, rent, utilities, school costs and household supplies. Those planned costs average about EGP 17,300, leaving a small monthly margin. A refrigerator repair in June costs EGP 7,800 and comes from her savings; EGP 7,800 remains designated for the next unexpected cost, against a gradual EGP 54,000 target.

Explore: how a gradual emergency fund absorbs an unexpected repair without making ordinary months feel unmanageable.

Opening cash: EGP 30,000 · Salary and spending are fictional; no household is assumed to have extra income.
