How to load a profile

A Few Clicks in Lightning.

Each built-in profile loads its accounts and sample activity into an empty Lightning profile.

  1. 1

    Open an Empty Profile

    Create a profile for sample data, or select an existing empty one. Samples cannot be added to a ledger that already has accounts.

  2. 2

    Choose a Story

    Select a profile, then choose Load sample. Lightning creates the accounts and adds the fictional transactions.

  3. 3

    Explore the Numbers

    Open Overview, Expenses, Budget, Investments or Cash planning to see the pattern behind that profile.

Pick a real question

The Number Only Matters in Context.

Each story starts with a different financial reality, then names the question worth exploring.

01

Cash flow

Amr · The Savvy Businessman

Amr works an engineering job in Cairo and earns EGP 14,500 a month. Alongside it, he occasionally helps businesses with technical freelance and outsourced projects. Those payments are unpredictable: some months bring nothing, while others add EGP 7,000–22,000. Rent, food, family support and bills keep arriving, and a few project costs need to be paid before the client settles.

ExploreWhich months run short, how much cash to keep back, and what changes if a client payment arrives late.Opening cash: 28,000 EGP · No debt assumed
02

Consistency

Abdelrahman · The Steady Engineer

Abdelrahman is a salaried engineer in Giza. His pay rises from EGP 32,000 to EGP 36,000 over the year, with a performance bonus. He keeps daily spending measured and invests around EGP 6,000–8,500 a month in a fictional equity fund. In June, a new laptop and an unplanned weekend push spending well above his normal month.

ExploreHow a one-off overspend affects his savings rate and steady investment habit over time.Opening cash: 60,000 EGP · Equity prices are illustrative
03

Safety

Menna · The Freelance Artist

Menna lives in Alexandria and illustrates for agencies and small businesses. Her invoices range from EGP 8,000 to EGP 33,000, with a few months where no client payment arrives. Materials, rent, transport and everyday costs continue between projects, so she draws on a modest cash reserve during quiet stretches. By October, only EGP 4,000 is set aside against a EGP 60,000 emergency-fund target.

ExploreHow large a buffer helps bridge late invoices and how to pace spending after a good month.Opening cash: 60,000 EGP · Income is irregular by design
04

Emergency reserve

Fatma · The Household Organiser

Fatma works in Cairo and earns EGP 18,000 a month. She keeps track of groceries, rent, utilities, school costs and household supplies. Those planned costs average about EGP 17,300, leaving a small monthly margin. A refrigerator repair in June costs EGP 7,800 and comes from her savings; EGP 7,800 remains designated for the next unexpected cost, against a gradual EGP 54,000 target.

ExploreHow a gradual emergency fund absorbs an unexpected repair without making ordinary months feel impossible.Opening cash: 30,000 EGP · Salary and spending are fictional
