Saving and Planning

How to Make a Personal Financial Plan in Egypt, Step by Step

A financial plan is not a forecast or a list of products. It is six small habits in the right order, each one using the figure from the one before.

Part of Budget Your Salary · Reading time: 6 minutes · Last updated: 2026-11-01

How do I make a financial plan on a salary in Egypt?Do six things in order: write down what you own and owe, track one month of spending, set a budget, build a reserve of 3 to 6 months of essentials, give one goal an amount and a date, then review once a month. The order matters, because each step uses the figure from the one before.

A financial plan does not start with where to put your money. It starts with knowing your own numbers. These six steps go in an order that works on a salary paid in EGP, because each one uses the figure from the step before.

1. Write Down What You Own and Owe

List what you own, each at what it would bring today and not what you paid: bank accounts, mobile wallets, cash at home, a bank certificate, gold, dollars and shares. Then list what you owe: the instalments still to pay, a card balance, money you borrowed. What you own less what you owe is your net worth. It is your starting line, and it is fine if it is small.

A Worked Example

Bank accounts and wallets38,000
Bank certificate60,000
Gold at home25,000
Dollar account, counted in EGP30,000
Instalments still owed−18,000
Net worth135,000 EGP

The figures are for illustration, so use your own.

Read how gold, certificates and shares are counted in your net worth.

2. Track One Month of Spending

For one month, write down every pound that leaves: cash, card, wallet and bank transfer. Don’t change how you spend yet. You are only looking. Bank text messages and a statement from your bank cover most of it, and a note on your phone covers cash. Here is how to track expenses without typing every one.

Then list the costs that come once or twice a year: school fees, Ramadan and Eid, summer, a car licence, a yearly insurance payment. Divide each by 12 and add the result to your monthly costs. School fees of 18,000 EGP a year are 1,500 EGP a month, even in the months you pay nothing.

3. Set a Budget From What You Saw

Now the month you tracked becomes a plan. Put fixed costs first: rent, instalments, bills and the yearly costs from step 2, divided by 12. Give each variable category a ceiling. What is left is yours to spend or save. If it is less than you expected, the plan is telling you something true. These guides show it line by line: building a monthly budget and splitting a 20,000 EGP salary.

4. Build a Small Reserve First

Before any goal, hold back a reserve of 3 to 6 months of essential spending. Without it, the first surprise can push you to borrow or to sell something at a bad moment. Start with a small milestone, even one month of essentials, and build from there. The emergency fund guide shows how to work out the number and where to keep it.

5. Give One Goal an Amount and a Date

A goal is something you can name, with an amount and a date: a flat deposit, school fees, a wedding. Pick one. Divide the amount by the months you have.

One Goal: 60,000 EGP in 24 Months

Months to get there24
Set aside a month2,500 EGP

Choose where the money sits after you know the date. Money you need in 12 months and money you won’t touch for ten years don’t belong in the same place. Compare the terms, the fees and the risks of each option: gold and dollar prices move, a share can be priced low just when you need it, and a certificate broken early can cost part of its return. A gameya fits too: count what you pay in each month, and the month you receive the lump sum.

6. Review Once a Month

Once a month, sit down for about 30 minutes. Update what you own and owe, compare spending with the budget, and check the goal. Change one thing, not everything. A plan that moves a little each month beats a perfect one you drop after two. That routine is the one in How It Works.

The Short Version

Know your numbers, see one month, budget it, hold a reserve, name one goal and review monthly. A plan is not a prediction. It is a way to make the next decision with the numbers in front of you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an adviser to start a financial plan?

No. The six steps need only your own numbers. An adviser can help with one specific decision, such as insurance or tax, but can’t do steps 1 and 2 for you. Lightning doesn’t tell you what to buy.

Should I clear my instalments before I save for a goal?

Put every instalment in the plan first, with what is left and the date it ends. Keep a small reserve so a surprise doesn’t add a new debt, then decide with the numbers in front of you. The right order depends on the terms of each instalment, which only you can see.

Where does a gameya fit in a financial plan?

A gameya is a monthly payment you commit to and a lump sum you receive in a known month. Put both in the plan, and fix the month you will be paid before you rely on it for a goal.

What if something breaks the plan in the middle of the month?

Change one line, not the whole plan. For a real emergency, use the reserve, write down why, and add a line to put it back. A broken month is information: it shows a cost you hadn’t planned for.
